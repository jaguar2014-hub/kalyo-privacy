Privacy Policy

This document explains which data leaves your device, when, and why.

Version 1.0.0

1. The short version

Kalyo has no account system and no server. Your diary is written to storage on your own device and stays there. Nothing is uploaded in the background, there is no analytics SDK in the app, there is no crash-reporting SDK, and there is no advertising. Data leaves your device only in the specific situations listed in section 3, each one triggered by something you do, and each one sending the minimum needed to answer that one request.

2. What is stored on your device

The app stores the following locally, in the application's own private storage:

  • Profile — height, weight, age, sex, activity level, and the goal you

set. These are the inputs to the calorie and nutrient target calculation; the app cannot produce a target without them.

  • Diary entries — the meals and foods you logged, with their quantities,

units, timestamps and the nutrient values resolved for them, including the ingredients of any composite dish.

  • Weight entries and activity entries — the values and dates you

recorded.

  • Goals and settings — water, step and sleep goals; notification

preferences; theme; interface language; which food sources are enabled; your preferred nutrient table; whether advanced mode is on.

  • Onboarding and consent state — how far through the setup you are, and

the record of your acceptance of the notice, Terms of Use and Privacy Policy: the version of each document you accepted, the time you accepted it, the app version, your interface language, and your age confirmation.

  • Google account identity, if you sign in — the identifier and profile

details Google returns for the signed-in account, kept so the app can show which account backup is attached to.

  • The backup encryption key, if you use Google Drive backup — a 256-bit

key generated on the device and held in the platform's secure key storage (the iOS Keychain, or Android Keystore-backed encrypted preferences).

Step counts, when you enable the step counter, are read from the device's own sensors through the operating system. They are not sent anywhere.

One photo is also saved to your device's photo gallery. When you take a meal photo with the camera, the app saves that photo to your gallery so it stays on your phone after the meal is logged. This happens automatically, without a separate confirmation, and the app asks for the gallery permission the first time. That copy is outside the app's own storage: it is a normal photo, it can be seen by any other app that has permission to read your gallery, and it is removed only when you delete it yourself. The meal entry in the app keeps its own copy in the app's private storage, where it is covered by everything above.

3. What leaves your device, and when

Each item below happens only as a direct result of an action you take. None of them runs in the background.

3.1 Meal photo recognition — MiniMax

When you photograph a meal and ask Kalyo to recognise it, the photo or photos (up to six, resized to at most 1,024 pixels on the long edge) are sent to MiniMax's Anthropic-compatible API endpoint at api.minimax.io, together with a text instruction asking the model to identify the foods and the interface language you want the names in. The model's reply is the list of candidate foods you then confirm or correct. No profile data, diary history, weight or account identity is included in the request. The same provider and endpoint are used when a dish is decomposed into its ingredients, which sends the dish's name as text.

3.2 Voice entry transcription — Groq, and OpenAI as a fallback

When you dictate an entry, the audio recording is uploaded for transcription. It goes first to Groq's transcription endpoint at api.groq.com. If that request fails or no Groq key is configured, the same recording is sent to OpenAI's transcription endpoint at api.openai.com. In both cases the upload is the audio file and the language hint, nothing else. If neither service is available the app tells you transcription is unavailable and asks you to type the entry instead.

The text that comes back is then sent to MiniMax's Anthropic-compatible API endpoint at api.minimax.io, together with an instruction asking for the foods and quantities in strict JSON. The request contains the dictated sentence — what you said, as text — and nothing about you: no profile, no diary history, no weight, no account identity. MiniMax replies with the list of foods and quantities, which you then confirm or correct.

3.3 Matching a recognised dish to a food table — MiniMax

When the app has a dish to place — the name it recognised from a photo, or the phrase it parsed from your voice — it asks MiniMax at api.minimax.io which of several candidate entries from the built-in food tables is the same food. The request contains the dish's name (in English and French, when both are known) and the shortlist of candidate names from the tables. It contains no profile data, no diary history, no weight and no account identity. MiniMax replies with the index of the matching candidate, or with nothing if no candidate is the same food. The decision is made to avoid a wrong entry slipping into your diary; you always see and confirm what was added.

3.4 Food search — USDA FoodData Central

Kalyo bundles a subset of USDA FoodData Central in the app. When a search needs a food outside that subset, the search text is sent as a query to the USDA FoodData Central API at api.nal.usda.gov. The request contains the words you typed and nothing about you.

3.5 Barcode scanning — Open Food Facts

When you scan a packaged product, the barcode number is sent to the Open Food Facts API at world.openfoodfacts.org to look up the product. The request contains the barcode and nothing else. Results are cached on the device so the same barcode is not looked up twice.

3.6 Google Sign-In

If you choose to sign in, authentication is handled by Google's sign-in library. Google receives the sign-in request and returns an identity token and basic profile information to the app. Your diary is not part of this exchange.

3.7 Google Drive backup

If you enable backup, Kalyo writes a file to a folder named Kalyo in your own Google Drive. Before anything is uploaded, your data is serialised to JSON, compressed with gzip, and encrypted on the device with AES-256-GCM. The upload contains only the encrypted bytes, the initialisation vector, the algorithm and format identifiers, a random key identifier, and a timestamp. Google therefore stores a file it cannot read. The app requests only the drive.file OAuth scope, which gives it access to files it created itself and no access to anything else in your Drive.

The encryption key never leaves the device and there is no escrowed copy. That has a consequence you should plan around: a backup can only be decrypted on the device that made it, so reinstalling the app or changing phones leaves existing backups unreadable.

3.8 Food search — Nutritionix

Kalyo can also search Nutritionix, the food database at trackapi.nutritionix.com. When it is enabled, the words you typed are sent to that endpoint as a search query, together with an application id and an API key in the request headers. The request contains nothing about you: no profile, no diary history, no weight, no account identity. Nutritionix receives the search text and answers with food names and nutrition facts.

This provider is not enabled in the build you are using — no Nutritionix credentials are configured, so the app never contacts it. It is described here because the code can call it, and this policy describes what the app can do rather than only what happens to be switched on today.

3.9 Other sources

The ANSES-CIQUAL, Swiss FSVO, Australian AFCD, Lebanese and Bahraini food composition tables are embedded in the app. Searching them involves no network request at all. The MET coefficients used to estimate calories burned during an activity are likewise part of the app.

4. What Kalyo does not do

To be explicit about the absences, because they are as much a part of this policy as the flows above:

  • There is no analytics or product-telemetry SDK in the app. No screen

views, taps, sessions or usage events are collected or transmitted.

  • There is no crash-reporting SDK. The app does not contain Sentry,

Firebase Crashlytics, Bugsnag or any equivalent, and crash reports are not sent to us. Diagnostic messages the app writes are written to the device log only.

  • There is no advertising and no advertising identifier is read.
  • There is no tracking across apps or websites, and no data is sold,

rented or shared with data brokers.

  • There is no Kalyo server, so there is no server-side profile of you to

request, correct or delete.

5. Legal basis and purpose

Where the EU General Data Protection Regulation or an equivalent regime applies, the processing described in section 3 rests on your consent, given by accepting this policy and by choosing to use the feature in question, and on the necessity of the processing to deliver the function you asked for. Each flow serves one purpose only: recognising a photo, transcribing a recording, resolving a food, or storing a backup you asked for. No flow is used for profiling, for advertising, or for any secondary purpose.

6. Retention

Data on your device is kept until you delete it — individual entries from the diary, or everything at once through the delete option in settings. Uninstalling the app removes its local storage. Backups you wrote to Google Drive stay in your Drive until you delete them there; Kalyo keeps one file per day plus a latest file, and a same-day backup replaces the previous one rather than accumulating copies.

We retain nothing, because we receive nothing. Data sent to the third-party services in section 3 is retained according to those providers' own policies, which we do not control: Google, MiniMax, Groq, OpenAI, USDA and Open Food Facts each publish their own terms.

7. Your rights

Because your data is on your device and not on a server, the rights that would normally require a request to us are things you can do directly in the app. You can see everything stored about you by browsing the diary and the profile. You can correct any entry by editing it. You can export your data, and you can delete all of it. Deletion is immediate and local, and it is not reversible — export first if you want a copy.

If you believe your rights have been infringed, you may lodge a complaint with your national data protection authority.

8. Children

Kalyo is for adults. You must confirm you are 18 or over before reaching the diary. We do not knowingly process the data of anyone under 18, and the app has no feature directed at children.

9. Changes to this policy

If this policy changes materially, its version number is raised and the app asks you to read and accept the new version before you continue using it. The version you accepted, and the date you accepted it, are recorded on your device and shown in the app's legal section.

10. Governing language

This policy is published in several languages as a convenience. If there is any discrepancy between translations, the English version governs.
